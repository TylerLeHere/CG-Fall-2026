# CSC 305 final exam

## Instructor’s guidance shared November 24

- The final focuses on understanding concepts and algorithms. Expect primarily short written explanations, with questions that can ask you to describe an algorithm, compare techniques, or work through mathematical or geometric reasoning.
- Nontrivial equations needed for a question will be supplied. Be able to interpret the symbols, recognize the assumptions, substitute values, and work through a basic derivation.
- A labelled diagram can help communicate the geometry or the order of operations.
- Review the lecture slides and the recordings from a previous offering under **Brightspace → Content**. Use the assignments to revisit how and why the techniques work.
- The instructor plans to go through example questions during the **November 30 review lecture**.

Lecture 7 gives examples of open-ended prompts on page 2. The course materials and announcement establish the format. The practice durations and scoring in this folder are study tools. [Spatial Data Structures](<../slides/07 - Spatial Data Structures.pdf>)

| Prompt | What a complete short answer does |
| --- | --- |
| Explain an algorithm | States the input, orders the key steps, explains the condition that makes them work, and gives the output. |
| Compare techniques | Uses the same criteria for both methods and gives a concrete case where the difference matters. |
| Apply a supplied equation or derive a result | Defines the relevant symbols, shows the main substitution or rearrangement, and interprets or checks the result. |
| Sketch supporting geometry | Labels the essential points, directions, axes, or intervals and ties them to the explanation. |

The course overview says that the exam is in person, counts for 50% of the course grade, and must be passed to pass the course. The introductory lecture assigns the exam to level A, which requires completing it independently without AI.
